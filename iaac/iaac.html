International Astronomy and Astrophysics Competition (IAAC)

The International Astronomy and Astrophysics Competition (IAAC) is an online science competition for middle school/college students around the world who are interested in astronomy, astrophysics, and the universe. Some of TanXG fellows are IAAC ambassadors, and we hope to introduce this opportunity to more young people, share useful preparation resources, and encourage students to explore astronomy beyond the standard classroom.

About IAAC

IAAC provides astronomy and astrophysics problems that combine scientific reasoning, calculation, and curiosity-driven learning. The competition is designed to be accessible internationally: students can participate online and work through challenging problems with only basic tools and an internet connection.

For students who enjoy physics, mathematics, space science, or scientific problem solving, IAAC can be a good starting point for building confidence in astronomy and discovering deeper topics such as stellar physics, orbital motion, observational astronomy, and cosmology.

For New Participants

What You Need

A basic background in physics and mathematics is helpful. More importantly, you should be willing to read carefully, think quantitatively, and learn new astronomical ideas while solving problems.

How to Prepare

Review mechanics, gravitation, light, basic statistics, and introductory astronomy. Try to explain your solution steps clearly rather than only writing final numerical answers.

Why Join

IAAC is not only about awards. It is also a chance to test your interest in astronomy, develop problem-solving habits, and connect classroom physics with real questions about the universe.
